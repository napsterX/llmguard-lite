# Code of Conduct

## 1. Purpose

LLMGuard Lite is infrastructure software built in public. The quality of that software depends directly on the quality of the community that builds and maintains it — which means clear expectations matter as much here as they do in any professional engineering environment.

This Code of Conduct defines the standards for participation across all LLMGuard Lite community spaces. It applies to everyone: first-time contributors, long-time collaborators, and project maintainers alike. The goal is a community where people can contribute effectively, disagree productively, and trust that the space is managed consistently and fairly.

---

## 2. Expected behavior

All participants are expected to:

- **Communicate with precision and respect.** Technical disagreements are welcome; personal criticism is not. Critique the idea, the implementation, or the approach — not the person.
- **Assume good faith.** Most misunderstandings in open-source communities are exactly that. Start from the assumption that others are trying to help.
- **Welcome contributors at all levels.** A question that seems obvious to an experienced contributor is a genuine blocker for someone earlier in their journey. Respond to questions with the same patience you would want when you are the one who does not know.
- **Accept feedback gracefully.** Code review exists to improve the software. Feedback on a pull request is not a judgement of the author's worth as an engineer.
- **Give credit generously.** Acknowledge others' ideas and prior work. In open-source infrastructure, attribution is part of the social contract.
- **Stay on topic.** Community spaces are for collaboration on LLMGuard Lite. Keep discussions relevant to the software, its governance, and its community.
- **Respect privacy.** Do not share others' personal information without explicit consent. This is especially important in a project whose purpose includes data protection.

---

## 3. Unacceptable behavior

The following are not tolerated in any community space:

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained personal criticism in any form
- Discriminatory language or jokes related to gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, ethnicity, nationality, religion, age, or level of experience
- Unsolicited personal advice, commentary on appearance, or romantic or sexual advances
- Deliberate misrepresentation of another person's words or positions
- Publishing others' private information — including email addresses or location data — without permission
- Sustained disruption of discussions, issues, or pull requests
- Retaliatory behavior toward someone who has filed a conduct report or participated in an investigation
- Any behavior that a reasonable person would consider unprofessional or unwelcome in a collaborative technical environment

---

## 4. Enforcement responsibilities

Project maintainers are responsible for defining, communicating, and consistently enforcing these standards across all community spaces. Responsibilities include:

- Responding to conduct reports in a timely and confidential manner
- Making clear, documented decisions about reported behavior
- Applying consequences proportionate to the severity and context of each incident
- Updating enforcement guidelines when community needs evolve

Maintainers who do not uphold this Code of Conduct may be removed from their role by agreement of the remaining active maintainers.

---

## 5. Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all community spaces managed by the LLMGuard Lite project, including:

- GitHub Issues, pull requests, and code review comments
- GitHub Discussions
- Commit messages and repository documentation
- Any communication channel officially listed in the project's README or CONTRIBUTING guide

It also applies when an individual is representing the project in external spaces — for example, presenting at a conference, writing a public post about the project, or acting in an official capacity on a mailing list or forum.

The Code of Conduct does not extend to purely personal conduct in unrelated spaces unless that conduct creates a direct safety risk for a community member or materially undermines trust in the project.

---

## 6. Reporting process

If you witness or experience behavior that violates this Code of Conduct, report it to the project maintainers at:

**conduct@hachira.com**

All reports are treated as confidential. The report will be reviewed only by the maintainers responsible for handling conduct issues, and details will not be shared beyond that group without the reporter's permission except where legally required.

**What to include in a report:**

- A description of what happened, including relevant context
- Where it occurred (a link to the specific issue, comment, or discussion is helpful)
- When it occurred
- Any other information you think is relevant

You do not need to have a complete account of events. If you witnessed part of an incident or are uncertain whether something qualifies, report it anyway and let the maintainers assess it.

Reports submitted in bad faith — fabricated or deliberately distorted to harm another community member — are themselves a violation of this Code of Conduct and will be treated accordingly.

---

## 7. Enforcement guidelines

Maintainers will assess each report individually. The following guidelines describe the range of responses, scaled to the nature and severity of the behavior.

### Correction

**Used for:** A single instance of language or behavior that is unprofessional or mildly disruptive but not part of a pattern.

**Response:** A private written note from a maintainer explaining why the behavior was problematic and what is expected going forward. No public action is taken.

### Warning

**Used for:** A clear violation of community standards, or a pattern of minor incidents despite a prior correction.

**Response:** A formal written warning that specifies the behavior in question and the consequences of further incidents. The individual may be asked to refrain from interaction in specific spaces for a defined period. Continuing the behavior after a warning is grounds for a temporary or permanent ban.

### Temporary suspension

**Used for:** A serious or sustained violation, including behavior that makes other community members feel unsafe or unwelcome.

**Response:** A temporary prohibition on participation in community spaces. The duration is proportionate to the severity of the incident. The individual is informed of the terms required to return to participation.

### Permanent removal

**Used for:** Repeated violations despite prior consequences, or a single incident of severe harm — including targeted harassment, coordinated disruption, or the intentional exposure of a community member's private information.

**Response:** Permanent removal from all community spaces with no pathway to reinstatement.

---

## 8. Resolution process

When a report is received, maintainers will:

1. **Acknowledge** the report within **48 hours** to confirm receipt.
2. **Review** the report, gather additional context if needed, and assess it against these guidelines.
3. **Decide** on a response within **7 days** of the acknowledgement. Complex cases may take longer; the reporter will be kept informed if that occurs.
4. **Communicate** the outcome to the reporter. Where an action has been taken, the affected party will also be notified of the decision and its basis — without identifying the reporter.
5. **Document** the decision internally for consistency in future assessments.

Anyone who disagrees with a conduct decision may appeal by replying to the decision notification with new information or a reasoned argument. Appeals are reviewed by a maintainer who was not involved in the original decision.

---

## 9. Maintainer commitments

In addition to applying this Code of Conduct to others, maintainers commit to holding themselves to the same standards:

- Maintainers will model the expected behavior in all public interactions within this project
- Maintainers will recuse themselves from enforcement decisions where they have a personal conflict of interest
- Maintainers will not use access to conduct reports to influence technical decisions or project governance
- Maintainers will review and update this document as the community evolves, incorporating feedback from contributors
